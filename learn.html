Module 1

Banking basics

A bank does two main things: it takes deposits from people and gives loans to others. The difference between the interest it earns on loans and the interest it pays on deposits is its main income.

CASACurrent Account + Savings Account deposits. Cheap money for the bank, so every branch pushes CASA growth.
Term deposits (FD/RD)Money kept for a fixed period at a fixed rate. Costlier for the bank than CASA.
Retail assetsLoans to individuals: home, personal, vehicle, gold, LAP.
MSME & agriLoans to small businesses and farmers, including KCC, working capital and term loans.

Bank vs NBFC vs Small Finance Bank

  • Bank: can take all kinds of deposits, including savings and current accounts, and run payment systems.
  • NBFC: lends money but generally cannot offer savings or current accounts. Often faster and more flexible with customers banks find harder to serve.
  • Small Finance Bank (SFB): a bank focused on small borrowers, small businesses and under-served areas.

Remember: in interviews for branch roles, expect questions on CASA growth, cross-selling, and customer service, not just targets.

Quick check ▸ Why do banks love CASA?

Because it is the cheapest source of funds. Lower cost of funds means better margins on every loan.

Module 2

Loans you should know

Home Loan (HL)To buy or build a house. Long tenure, the property is the security.
Loan Against Property (LAP)Borrow against a property you already own, for business or personal needs. Popular with self-employed customers.
Gold LoanQuick loan against gold jewellery. Short tenure, fast disbursal, the gold is kept as security.
Business Loan (BL)For running or growing a business. Can be secured or unsecured.
Personal Loan (PL)Unsecured loan for any personal purpose, so it depends on income and credit history.
Kisan Credit Card (KCC)Revolving credit for farmers' crop and allied needs, sized on land and cropping pattern.

Life of a loan

1. Sourcing: finding customers (direct, DSA/connector, branch walk-in)
2. Login: application and documents submitted
3. Credit appraisal: income, repayment ability, credit history
4. Legal & technical: for secured loans, title and property value checks
5. Sanction: approval of amount, rate and tenure
6. Disbursal: money released
7. Collections: EMIs collected; follow-up if missed
Quick check ▸ What is the difference between HL and LAP?

A home loan is taken to buy or build a house. LAP is taken against a property you already own, usually for business or other needs.

Module 3

Credit & CIBIL, simply explained

Credit appraisal answers one question: can and will this customer repay?

CharacterPast repayment behaviour, seen in the credit bureau report.
CapacityIncome vs existing EMIs: can they afford one more?
CapitalThe customer's own money in the deal or business.
CollateralSecurity offered, such as property or gold.
ConditionsPurpose of the loan and the business or economic situation.

Terms you'll hear every day

  • FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio): share of monthly income already going into EMIs. Lower is safer.
  • LTV (Loan to Value): loan amount as a percentage of the security's value. Lower LTV means more cushion for the lender.
  • Credit score: a number from a credit bureau (CIBIL is the best known) that reflects repayment history. Late payments pull it down.
  • DPD (Days Past Due): how many days an EMI is overdue.
  • NPA: a loan where payment has been overdue for a long period (generally 90+ days).

Tip: know your own product's numbers: typical ticket size, approval rate and common rejection reasons. Interviewers love specifics.

Module 4

KYC, AML & fair practice

  • KYC (Know Your Customer): verifying who the customer is (identity and address) before opening an account or giving a loan.
  • AML (Anti-Money Laundering): watching for suspicious transactions, such as unusual cash deposits or transfers that don't match a customer's profile, and reporting them as per rules.
  • Fair practices: clear terms, no hidden charges, polite recovery. Mis-selling and harsh collection hurt customers and the institution.
  • Data privacy: customer documents and data must be handled carefully and shared only for valid purposes.

Interview angle: "What would you do if a customer's documents look tampered?" Answer: don't process; escalate to credit/compliance as per policy.

Module 5

Insurance & wealth basics

Life insurancePays the family if the insured person dies. Term plans are pure protection.
Health insuranceCovers hospital and medical costs.
General insuranceMotor, home, shop, crop: protects assets.
BancassuranceBanks selling insurance products through their branches, in partnership with insurers.
Mutual fundsPooled investments managed by professionals, from equity (higher risk) to debt (lower risk).
Risk profilingMatching products to a customer's age, goals and risk appetite. The heart of ethical wealth selling.

Golden rule: recommend what suits the customer, explain risks clearly, and never promise guaranteed returns on market-linked products.

Module 6

Career scope in BFSI

BFSI offers clear growth paths, especially in sales, credit and branch banking.

SalesRelationship Officer → Sales Manager → Branch Manager → Cluster / Area Head
CreditCredit Officer → Credit Manager → Area / Regional Credit Manager
OperationsOps Executive → Branch Ops Manager → Regional Ops
CollectionsCollection Officer → Collection Manager → Area Collection Manager
InsuranceRelationship Associate → Bancassurance Manager → Area Manager

Skills that get you hired

  • Product knowledge and your own numbers
  • Local market network (customers, DSAs, builders, jewellers)
  • Clear communication in Punjabi, Hindi and English
  • Integrity: clean files and ethical selling

Quick reference

Glossary

DSA: Direct Selling Agent who sources loan customers for a lender.
Ticket size: average loan amount per customer.
Login: a loan file formally submitted for processing.
TAT: turnaround time from login to disbursal.
Sanction letter: lender's written approval with terms.
Foreclosure: repaying a loan fully before its tenure ends.
Top-up: extra loan on an existing running loan.
Balance transfer: moving a loan to another lender for a better rate.
CTC: Cost To Company, your total yearly salary package.
Notice period: time you must serve before leaving a job.

These notes are simplified for learning and interview preparation. Rules and product terms differ across lenders and change over time; always follow your employer's policies and the regulator's latest guidelines.
